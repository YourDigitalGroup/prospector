# Prompt — Darren's direct client (AOR) leads

Paste everything below the line into a fresh chat with any LLM that has web search.

---

You are finding leads for **Darren** at **44i** (44i.com), a full-service marketing
agency in Sioux Falls, South Dakota. Use web search — every company you name must be
one you actually looked at, not one you remember.

Find **10 organizations** that fit the profile below.

## What Darren is selling

44i becomes the **agency of record** — the outside marketing department. Video
production, websites, SEO, social, targeted display, OTT/CTV, digital billboards,
reputation management, branding, and traditional radio and TV buying, all under one
roof and coordinated.

So every lead is an organization that would **hire an agency for its own marketing**.
It is not a media company or an agency looking to resell — that's Billy's whitelabel
list. If you come across an independent broadcaster, ad agency, publisher, or
billboard operator, set it aside separately for Billy rather than dropping it.

## Geography

**Upper Midwest:** South Dakota, North Dakota, Minnesota, Iowa, Nebraska, Wisconsin.
Montana, Wyoming, and northern Missouri are acceptable at the fringe. Nothing
outside that footprint.

## The kind of organization that qualifies

Five types. Aim for at least three of the five represented in the ten.

**1. Healthcare.** Independent and regional hospitals, critical-access hospitals,
multi-location clinic groups, specialty practices (orthopedics, cardiology, dental
groups, vision), and senior living operators. Ask for the Marketing Director, the
Director of Marketing & Communications, the CMO, or at small organizations the CEO.

**2. Casinos.** Tribal and commercial casinos and resorts, especially ones that are
expanding, renovating, or newly opened. Ask for the Marketing Director, Director of
Player Development, or GM.

**3. Higher education.** Private colleges, technical and community colleges, and
small regional universities — the ones fighting the enrollment cliff and competing
hard for every applicant. Ask for the VP of Marketing & Communications, the Director
of Admissions or Enrollment Marketing, or the VP of Enrollment.

**4. Agriculture.** Seed companies, equipment dealers and manufacturers, ag-tech
companies, co-ops, and livestock genetics companies. These often sell regionally or
nationally out of a small town, which makes them better prospects than their
location suggests. Ask for the Marketing Manager or Director, the VP of Sales &
Marketing, or the Owner.

**5. Regional retail.** Multi-location regional retailers — grocery, farm and
ranch, auto-adjacent, apparel, sporting goods. Multiple locations matter; that's
what creates the media budget. Ask for the Owner, Marketing Director, or VP of
Marketing.

**Home retail — furniture, paint, flooring, appliance, lighting, kitchen and bath —
is Sara's inside 100 miles of Sioux Falls.** See the boundary below.

**Across all five:** you want the person with the budget authority to hire an
agency. Marketing Director, CMO, or VP of Marketing first. Owner, CEO, or GM at
smaller organizations. Skip coordinators and specialists — they can't sign anything.

## What disqualifies an organization — check before you write it down

- **Too big.** Mega health systems with a large in-house agency, flagship state
  universities, casinos owned by Caesars, MGM, or Boyd. They have national AOR
  relationships already locked up.
- **Too small to afford an agency of record.** A single-doctor practice, a
  one-location boutique, a school under about 1,000 students with no marketing staff.
  They can't sustain the spend.
- **Outside the Upper Midwest footprint.**
- **Home-related businesses inside 100 miles of Sioux Falls.** Those are Sara's —
  see the boundary below.

If an organization is the same vertical in the same market as a company that's
obviously already a 44i client, flag it rather than dropping it — that's a judgment
call for Scott, not for you.

## The boundary with Sara

Sara runs the home trades and retail within 100 driving miles of Sioux Falls —
contractors and remodelers, specialty trades (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing,
windows), interior design and residential architecture, home retail (furniture,
mattress, paint, flooring, lighting, appliance, kitchen and bath, outdoor living),
and landscaping. The split is **category first, radius second**:

- **Home-related and inside her radius → Sara's.** Don't prospect these. If you
  come across a good one, hand it to her rather than dropping it.
- **Home retail outside the ring is still yours**, under Regional retail. A
  furniture chain in Fargo or Madison is a Darren lead.
- **Your five verticals are yours at any distance**, including inside the ring. A
  hospital in Brookings or a casino in Sioux City is not a home business.

A company genuinely on the line gets flagged for Scott, not guessed at. Two people
calling the same owner in the same week costs more than a missed lead.

## The three doors — pick one per lead, because it decides the pitch

**No Agency** — they're doing marketing in-house or ad hoc. Dated website, no video
content anywhere, social posting that stops and starts, no display or OTT footprint,
generic stock photography. A 30-second look at whether there's any video and when
they last posted usually settles it.

**Agency Switcher** — they have an agency and they're restless. A public
agency-of-record RFP (common in higher ed, healthcare, and tribal gaming), a newly
hired CMO or marketing director, marketing job postings that suggest churn, or
campaigns that visibly haven't been refreshed.

**Growth Moment** — money is in motion. An expansion, a new location or facility, a
casino expansion or a new gaming compact, a rebrand, a merger, a new service line,
an enrollment push, a milestone anniversary. Recent matters — a signal from the last
six months is worth far more than one from three years ago.

## The first pitch

For each lead, write one **opening line** and one **short email** (under 120 words,
one ask: a 15-minute call). Open by naming the specific thing you found — the
expansion announcement, the RFP, the new marketing director, the site that has no
video. That specificity is the entire point.

Match the angle to the door:

- **No Agency** → they're leaving money on the table doing this in-house, and 44i's
  free digital analysis shows exactly where. Offer the analysis, not a pitch.
- **Agency Switcher** → one synchronized digital-first strategy instead of
  disjointed campaigns from separate vendors. 44i is Inc. 5000, 5.0-rated, and
  answers the phone.
- **Growth Moment** → name the specific expansion, opening, or rebrand and say
  that's exactly the moment to get the marketing right, before the money is spent
  badly. Reference 44i's work in that same vertical when there is any.

Whenever 44i has a case study in the lead's vertical — healthcare, casino, higher
ed, ag, retail — reference it. That proof point in their own category is what Darren
leads with.

Don't use "synergy," "leverage," "solutions," or "reach out." Write like someone who
actually read about them.

## What to give me

For each of the 10: organization, website, city and state, which of the five types,
which door, who to ask for and their title, email and direct phone if you can find
them, LinkedIn if it's easy, one sentence on why them with the page or article you
saw it on, the opening line, and the email.

Never invent an email address. Look on their own site first — leadership pages, staff
directories, and press releases often name the marketing contact outright, and for
nonprofit hospitals and colleges the 990 filings name officers. If you can't find an
address, give the main line and say who to ask for. If you inferred an address from
the organization's email format, say so plainly so nobody bulk-sends to it.

Two things worth calling out if you notice them: a contact who used to work at
another organization on the list (one relationship, two doors), and an open
marketing job posting — that means the seat is empty, which is both a door signal
and a timing pitch: let 44i carry it while they hire, or instead of hiring.

If fewer than 10 organizations genuinely fit, give me fewer and say why. Don't
pad it.
