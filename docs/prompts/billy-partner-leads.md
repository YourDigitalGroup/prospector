# Prompt — Billy's whitelabel partner leads

Paste everything below the line into a fresh chat with any LLM that has web search.

---

You are finding leads for **Billy** at **44i Digital** (44idigital.com), in Sioux
Falls, South Dakota. Use web search — every company you name must be one you
actually looked at, not one you remember.

Find **10 companies** that fit the profile below.

## What Billy is selling

44i Digital is a **whitelabel fulfillment partner**. A media company or ad agency
sells digital services to their own advertisers, under their own brand, and 44i does
the work behind the scenes — SEO, Google Ads, programmatic display, OTT/CTV,
geofencing, social advertising, websites, reputation management.

The partner keeps the client relationship and the margin. 44i's name never appears.

So every lead is a company that will **resell**. It is not a company that needs
marketing for itself. If you come across a hospital, casino, college, or retailer
that needs marketing, that's Darren's list, not Billy's — set it aside separately.

## The kind of company that qualifies

Four types, and nothing else:

**1. Independent TV and radio broadcasters.** Family-owned or independently owned.
One station up to a small cluster of a few markets. Their revenue comes mostly from
local direct advertisers — the car dealer, the furniture store, the bank — not from
national rep-firm business. Ask for the GM, Market Manager, Owner, VP of Sales, or
Director of Sales.

**2. Traditional ad agencies with a digital gap.** Full-service or creative shops
that still do brand work, print, and media buying but have no real digital
capability in-house. The tell is their own services page: if it lists creative,
branding, and media but not SEM, programmatic, or OTT, that's the gap. Ask for the
Owner, Principal, President, or Partner.

**3. Local publishers.** Community and regional newspapers, city and regional
magazines, niche print publications — the ones with a legacy print sales team who
are being asked to sell digital they don't have. Ask for the Publisher, GM, or
Advertising Director.

**4. Independent out-of-home operators.** Billboard and OOH companies, including
digital-billboard operators, that aren't part of a national chain. Ask for the
Owner, GM, or VP of Sales.

**Market size sweet spot:** metros of roughly 50,000 to 1,000,000 people. Big enough
to have real local advertisers, small enough that nobody built an in-house digital
department.

## What disqualifies a company — check before you write it down

- **Owned by a big group that already has in-house digital.** Look up the actual
  owner, don't guess from the call letters. Nexstar, Gray, Sinclair, Scripps,
  Tegna, iHeartMedia, Audacy, Cumulus, Townsquare, Saga, Hubbard, Gannett, Lee
  Enterprises, Hearst, McClatchy — all out.
- **Digital-first agencies.** If doing the digital work *is* their business, they're
  a competitor, not a partner.
- **Not in the United States.**

## The three doors — pick one per lead, because it decides the pitch

**Fresh Start** — they've never had a whitelabel partner. No digital services
listed anywhere, dated website, sales pages that talk only about spots or print
inches or boards.

**Frustrated Refugee** — they tried a provider and it went badly. A thin or
obviously templated digital services page, a "digital" tab that goes nowhere, signs
that the page was written by somebody else.

**Gap Filler** — they do some digital themselves but not all of it. They list social
media and websites but not programmatic, OTT, SEM, or geofencing. Or they say
they "partner with vendors." Or they're currently hiring a digital sales person.

## The first pitch

For each lead, write one **opening line** and one **short email** (under 120 words,
one ask: a 15-minute call). Open by naming the specific thing you noticed on their
site — that specificity is the entire point. Generic openers get deleted.

Match the angle to the door:

- **Fresh Start** → make it sound easy and low-risk. They resell, 44i fulfills,
  and there's a revenue guarantee for new partners, so trying it costs them nothing.
- **Frustrated Refugee** → lead with reliability, not features. A partner that
  actually answers the phone. Month-to-month, no lock-in, and the whole team is
  in Sioux Falls — they'll be talking to the people doing the work, not an account
  manager offshore.
- **Gap Filler** → lead with not stepping on their people. "We slot in around the
  team you already have — if you've got a social person, we don't touch social."
  Then name the specific service they're missing.

Don't use "synergy," "leverage," "solutions," or "reach out." Write like someone
who actually read their website.

## What to give me

For each of the 10: company, website, city and state, which of the four types, which
door, who to ask for and their title, email and phone if you can find them, one
sentence on why them with the page you saw it on, the opening line, and the email.

Never invent an email address. If you can't find one, give the main line and say who
to ask for — that's a useful answer. If you inferred an address from the company's
format, say so plainly so nobody bulk-sends to it.

If fewer than 10 companies genuinely fit, give me fewer and say why. Don't pad it.
