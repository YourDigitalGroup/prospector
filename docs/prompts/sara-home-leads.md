# Prompt — Sara's home trades and retail leads

Paste everything below the line into a fresh chat with any LLM that has web search.

---

You are finding leads for **Sara** at **44i** (44i.com), a full-service marketing agency in Sioux
Falls, South Dakota. Use web search — every company you name must be one you actually looked at,
not one you remember.

Find **10 businesses** that fit the profile below.

## What Sara is selling

44i runs the marketing for home-related businesses: local SEO and Google Business Profile, reviews
and reputation, paid search, targeted display and geofencing, OTT/CTV, video, and websites.

These buyers care about one thing — **lead flow**. The phone ringing in February. A pitch that opens
with brand strategy will lose to one that opens with where their calls are leaking.

## Territory — check this first, before anything else

**Within 100 driving miles of Sioux Falls, South Dakota.** That is roughly:

- Sioux Falls and its ring — Brandon, Harrisburg, Tea, Dell Rapids, Canton
- Siouxland — Sioux City, North Sioux City, Dakota Dunes, Le Mars, Sergeant Bluff
- I-29 north — Brookings, Madison, Flandreau, Watertown at the outer edge
- The James River valley — Mitchell, Yankton, Vermillion, Parkston
- Southwest Minnesota — Luverne, Pipestone, Worthington, Windom, Marshall
- Northwest Iowa — Rock Rapids, Sheldon, Spencer, Spirit Lake, Storm Lake

If the nearest location is outside that ring, it is not Sara's — however good it looks. Say so and
move on rather than stretching the radius.

## The kind of business that qualifies

Five types. Aim for at least three of the five in the ten.

**1. Remodeling and building.** Design-build remodelers, custom home builders, and general
contractors — the ones with a showroom or a real brand, not a truck and a phone number. Ask for the
Owner, President, or Marketing Manager.

**2. Specialty trades.** HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, siding and windows, insulation, garage
doors, water treatment. Ask for the Owner, GM, or Marketing Manager.

**3. Design and architecture.** Interior designers, kitchen and bath designers, residential
architects, home staging firms. Ask for the Owner, Principal, or Studio Director.

**4. Home retail.** Furniture, mattress, paint and decorating, flooring and tile, lighting,
appliance, window treatments, kitchen and bath showrooms, outdoor living. Ask for the Owner or
Marketing Director.

**5. Outdoor and exterior.** Landscaping and hardscape, lawn care, decks and fencing, pools and
spas, irrigation, seasonal and snow. Ask for the Owner or GM.

**Size sweet spot:** roughly $1M–$25M in revenue, 5–100 employees, with at least one physical
location or a branded fleet. Big enough to have a marketing budget, small enough to have nobody
whose actual job is marketing. That gap is the whole opportunity.

**In this vertical the owner usually is the buyer.** Owner or President first, then a Marketing
Manager where one genuinely exists, then the GM. A "marketing coordinator" at a 30-person contractor
is usually the person who posts on Facebook, not the person who can sign a contract.

## What disqualifies a business — check before you write it down

- **Outside the 100-mile ring.**
- **Anything that belongs to Darren.** Healthcare, casinos, higher education, agriculture, and
  non-home retail (grocery, farm and ranch, auto, apparel, sporting goods) are his, at any distance.
  If you come across a good one, set it aside for Darren rather than dropping it.
- **Sole operators** — one truck, no crew, no location. They cannot sustain a retainer.
- **National franchise locations** where marketing is run at corporate and the local owner has no
  budget authority. Flag rather than drop when the franchisee clearly does buy local media; some do.
- **Same trade and same town as an existing 44i client.** Flag it for Scott instead of guessing —
  exclusivity matters more here than in most verticals, because two roofers in Brandon are competing
  for the same roof.

## The three doors — pick one per lead, because it decides the pitch

**Lead-Starved** — demand dries up between seasons. No paid search presence, a thin or template
website, few or stale reviews, no landing pages, a site that says "call for a quote" and nothing
else.

**Growth Push** — money and capacity in motion. A second location or new showroom, a fleet expansion
or fresh truck wraps, an acquired competitor, aggressive crew and installer hiring, a new product
line or dealership.

**Referral Cliff** — established but undefended. Strong reviews and a long history, but a dated
site, no content, no ads, and revenue visibly tied to a couple of builder or realtor relationships.

A 60-second audit usually settles which: search the trade plus the town and see who is buying ads,
open the site on a phone, and read the last ten reviews and their dates.

## Where to look

State contractor and trade licensing boards for South Dakota, Iowa, Minnesota and Nebraska —
electrical, plumbing and mechanical rosters are public and list the business, the town, and often
the owner by name. The Home Builders Association of the Sioux Empire and local NARI and NAHB chapter
member lists. Chamber directories for each town in the ring. Manufacturer and supplier dealer
locators, which are how you find the paint, flooring, window and appliance dealers in a market. BBB
listings by category. Houzz, Angi and Google Maps for the review and recency picture.

**City building-permit records are the best single signal here.** They are public, they name the
contractor, and permit volume trending up is a Growth Push you can prove with a date. Sioux Falls,
Sioux City and Brookings all publish them.

## The first pitch

For each lead, write one **opening line** and one **short email** (under 120 words, one ask: a
15-minute call). Open by naming the specific thing you found — the new showroom, the permit run, the
reviews nobody sees, the competitor outbidding them on their own name. That specificity is the
entire point.

Match the angle to the door:

- **Lead-Starved** → they are relying on word of mouth to fill February. A free digital analysis
  shows exactly where the calls are leaking. Offer the analysis, not a pitch.
- **Growth Push** → name the specific new location, showroom, or crew hire and say the phone needs
  to be ringing before the trucks are paid for.
- **Referral Cliff** → their reviews are the best in town and almost nobody searching for that trade
  ever sees them. That is a fixable problem.

Owners in this vertical answer their own phones and have no patience for agency language. Don't use
"synergy," "leverage," "solutions," or "reach out." Write like a neighbour who looked them up.

## What to give me

For each of the 10: business, website, city and state, which of the five types, which door, who to
ask for and their title, email and phone if you can find them, one sentence on why them with the
page or record you saw it on, the opening line, and the email.

Never invent an email address. In this vertical the general inbox (`info@`, `office@`) is often
genuinely the way in and the office manager is the gatekeeper worth naming — say so when that is
the real route. A mobile number from a truck wrap or a Google Business Profile is a legitimate
business number here; record it as the direct phone. If you inferred an address from the company's
format, say so plainly so nobody bulk-sends to it.

Two things worth calling out if you notice them: **seasonality** — when is their slow quarter, since
that is when the pitch lands — and **hiring signals**, because crews being hired means capacity is
going up and the work has to be found to fill it.

If fewer than 10 businesses genuinely fit, give me fewer and say why. Don't pad it.
