# Home Prospector Loop — 44i (Sara)

Produce scored, enriched, send-ready prospective **direct clients** for 44i (44i.com) in the
home-related trades and retail. These are end clients where 44i runs the marketing — local SEO and
Google Business Profile, reviews and reputation, paid search, targeted display and geofencing,
OTT/CTV, video, and websites.

This is not the whitelabel reseller program (Billy's partner loop), and it is not Darren's client
loop. Sara's territory is defined by **category and radius**, and the boundary is deliberate: it is
what stops two people calling the same business.

## Territory — hard boundary, applied before anything else

**Within 100 driving miles of Sioux Falls, South Dakota.** That is roughly:

- Sioux Falls and its ring — Brandon, Harrisburg, Tea, Dell Rapids, Canton
- The Siouxland corridor — Sioux City, North Sioux City, Dakota Dunes, Le Mars, Sergeant Bluff
- North on I-29 — Brookings, Madison, Flandreau, Watertown at the outer edge
- The James River valley — Mitchell, Yankton, Vermillion, Parkston
- Southwest Minnesota — Luverne, Pipestone, Worthington, Windom, Marshall
- Northwest Iowa — Rock Rapids, Sheldon, Spencer, Spirit Lake, Storm Lake

If a company's nearest location is outside that ring, it is not Sara's, however good it looks. Say
so and move on rather than stretching the radius.

## Five qualifying verticals

| Vertical | Sweet spot | Decision-makers |
|---|---|---|
| Remodeling & building | Design-build remodelers, custom home builders, general contractors with a showroom or a real brand | Owner, President, Marketing Manager |
| Specialty trades | HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, siding & windows, insulation, garage doors, water treatment | Owner, GM, Marketing Manager |
| Design & architecture | Interior designers, kitchen & bath designers, residential architects, home staging firms | Owner, Principal, Studio Director |
| Home retail | Furniture, mattress, paint & decorating, flooring & tile, lighting, appliance, window treatments, kitchen & bath showrooms, outdoor living | Owner, Marketing Director |
| Outdoor & exterior | Landscaping and hardscape, lawn care, decks and fencing, pools and spas, irrigation, seasonal and snow | Owner, GM |

**Size sweet spot:** roughly $1M–$25M in revenue, 5–100 employees, with at least one physical
location or a branded fleet. Big enough to have a marketing budget, small enough to have nobody
whose actual job is marketing. That gap is the whole opportunity.

In this vertical the owner usually is the buyer. Go to the Owner or President first, then a
Marketing Manager where one exists, then the GM. A "marketing coordinator" at a 30-person
contractor is usually the person who posts on Facebook, not the person who can sign.

## Three buyer doors and their detectable signals

| Door | Signals |
|---|---|
| **Lead-Starved** (demand dries up between seasons) | No paid search presence, thin or template website, few or stale reviews, no landing pages, a site that says "call for a quote" and nothing else, no tracked phone number |
| **Growth Push** (money and capacity in motion) | A second location or new showroom, a fleet expansion or fresh truck wraps, an acquired competitor, aggressive crew and installer hiring, a new product line or dealership |
| **Referral Cliff** (established but undefended) | Strong reviews and a long history, but a dated site, no content, no ads, and revenue visibly tied to a couple of builder or realtor relationships |

Home services buy **lead flow**, not brand. A pitch that leads with brand strategy will lose to one
that leads with the phone ringing in February.

## Hard disqualifiers — apply before scoring

- Outside the 100-mile radius above
- **Anything in Darren's loop**: healthcare, casinos, higher education, agriculture, and non-home
  retail (grocery, farm and ranch, auto, apparel, sporting goods). If it is a hospital, a college, a
  casino, a seed company or a grocery chain, it is Darren's — hand it over rather than dropping it.
- Sole operators — one truck, no crew, no location. They cannot sustain a retainer.
- National franchise locations where marketing is controlled at corporate and the local owner has no
  budget authority. Flag rather than drop when the franchisee clearly does buy local media; some do.
- Existing 44i clients, or anything on the exclusion list supplied in the task
- **Competitive conflict**: same trade AND same town as a current 44i client. Flag it, do not
  silently drop it — Scott decides. Exclusivity matters more in a trade than in a hospital, because
  two roofers in Brandon are directly competing for the same roof.

## Fit score (0–100)

+25 vertical and radius match · +20 retainer capacity (size in the sweet spot, evidence of existing

email or mobile found · +10 growth-moment recency (signal less than 6 months old)

## Sourcing

State contractor and trade licensing boards for South Dakota, Iowa, Minnesota and Nebraska —
electrical, plumbing and mechanical licensing rosters are public and list the business, the town and
often the owner. The Home Builders Association of the Sioux Empire and the local NARI and NAHB
chapter member lists. Chamber of commerce directories for each town in the ring. Supplier and
manufacturer dealer locators, which are how you find the paint, flooring, window and appliance
dealers in a market. BBB accredited-business listings by category. Houzz, Angi and Google Maps for
the review and recency picture.

**City building-permit records are the best single signal in this loop.** They are public, they name
the contractor, and permit volume trending up is a Growth Push you can prove with a date. Sioux
Falls, Sioux City and Brookings all publish them.

A 60-second audit separates the doors: search the trade plus the town and see who is buying ads,
open the site on a phone, and read the last ten reviews and their dates.

## Contact enrichment — source hierarchy and confidence

1. **The company's own website** — About, Our Team, and Contact pages. Owner-run businesses name the
   owner far more often than corporates do → `verified`
2. **Licensing board records** — the licence holder is frequently the owner, with a business address
   and phone → `verified` for the name, and it is a dated official record
3. **Local news and trade press** — expansion stories, business-journal profiles and chamber
   newsletters quote owners by name → `verified` for the name, check the date
4. **Third-party databases** — triangulation only. Two independent sources agreeing on a name,
   title or email → `high`. Never treat one paywalled snippet as fact.
5. **Email-pattern inference** — a known domain and format makes an inferred address `pattern`,
   always labelled, never presented as verified.

Hard rules:

- **Never fabricate an email address.** If the handle cannot be found, give the main line and the
  route ("call and ask for the owner") instead.
- `pattern` addresses must be verified before any bulk send — bounces damage the sending domain.
- In this vertical the general inbox (`info@`, `office@`) is often genuinely the way in, and the
  office manager is the gatekeeper worth naming. Say so when that is the real route.
- A mobile number on a truck wrap or a Google Business Profile is a legitimate business number here
  in a way it would not be for a hospital. Record it as the direct phone.
- Fallback ladder: named owner → GM or office manager → main line with a named ask → the contact
  form, last.

Also capture, when you see it: **seasonality** (when is their slow quarter — that is when the pitch
lands), and **hiring signals** (crews being hired means capacity is going up and the work has to be
found to fill it).

## Door-matched opening hooks (44i.com positioning)

- **Lead-Starved** → "you're relying on word of mouth to fill February — a free digital analysis
  shows exactly where the calls are leaking"
- **Growth Push** → "[the specific new showroom / second location / crew hire] means you need the
  phone ringing before the trucks are paid for"
- **Referral Cliff** → "your reviews are the best in [town] and almost nobody searching for
  [trade] ever sees them — that's a fixable problem"

Keep every hook phone-readable and concrete. Owners in this vertical answer their own phones and
have no patience for agency language.
